No single person knows how to make a pencil.
Not the CEO of the pencil company. Not the smartest engineer alive. No one. The knowledge is scattered across millions of people who will never meet.
And that's the point.
Every product you touch, every service you use, exists because millions of strangers trusted each other to do their part. No one planned it. No one could.
The logger doesn't know the chemist. The miner never meets the painter. The sailor has no idea where the pencil ends up. Yet each one contributes something no one else can.
This is delegation at a scale no org chart could capture. Not top-down. Not designed. Emergent.
No genius designed this. No committee approved it.
A logger in Oregon, a miner in Chile, a chemist in Germany, a sailor in Singapore. Each one a specialist. Each one brilliant at their tiny piece. None of them coordinating. All of them connected by an invisible hand that no one controls and everyone depends on.
A pencil costs ten cents because a million people each did their tiny part.
That's not a flaw. That's civilization working.
